## 🇨🇳 China Moves India Hasn't Matched Yet

### 1. A five-year industrial plan that names RISC-V as national infrastructure

**What China did:** MIIT and NDRC's new five-year tech plan (announced 15 Sep) forecasts over 30 trillion yuan in related-sector revenue by 2030 and explicitly names RISC-V integrated circuits, BeiDou, batteries and perovskite tandem solar alongside general IC capability — the framing is "comprehensive progress across the entire integrated-circuit industry", i.e. escaping Western instruction-set and tooling dependence, not just building fabs.

**Where India stands:** India has twelve approved semiconductor units and roughly ₹1.64 lakh crore committed, with commercial production started at Micron, CG Semi and Kaynes — but that is overwhelmingly OSAT/packaging. The Tata-PSMC Dholera fab targets first silicon around December 2026 at 28-110nm, with full commissioning reported for 2028, and India has no leading-edge logic path before 2030+. India has a RISC-V effort (Shakti, VEGA) but nothing resembling a national-plan-level commitment to make it the default architecture.

**Why it matters:** The gap isn't fab count, it's *architectural sovereignty*. China is treating the instruction set and EDA layer as the strategic chokepoint; India is still treating assembly capacity as the win. For a software founder, the actionable read is the layer above: chip-design tooling and verification software is where a small Indian team can compete without capital, and Beijing's own EDA leader is publicly saying agentic AI is a "paradigm shift" in that workflow.

### 2. Agentic payments as an open standard, shipped across 1.5 billion accounts

**What China did:** Ant International open-sourced the Agentic Mobile Protocol, letting AI agents act as authenticated payers inside 10 Asian wallets — Alipay, AlipayHK, Starryblu, KakaoPay, Toss and others — across the Alipay+ network's 1.5 billion accounts, with card-network partners attached. Separately, China and Kazakhstan will launch cross-border QR payments by year-end, with Kazakhstan lining up 10 more such corridors from mid-2027.

**Where India stands:** UPI is the better *domestic* rail by most measures, and cross-border UPI is now live in ten countries (UAE, Singapore, France, Greece, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Bhutan, Mauritius, Qatar, Cambodia). So this is not a payments-infrastructure gap. The gap is narrower and sharper: there is no published Indian standard for **how an autonomous agent authenticates, holds a mandate, and spends** on a user's behalf. NPCI has mandates and AutoPay, but not an agent-identity protocol — and NPCI's own stated horizon for the full bilateral corridor network is 20-25 years.

**Why it matters:** Whoever's protocol becomes the default for agent-initiated payment captures the rent on every autonomous transaction in Asia. Ant just made theirs free to adopt, which is how standards win. If India ships agentic commerce on ad-hoc per-merchant integrations while Alipay+ ships one protocol, Indian agent startups will end up building to Ant's spec in Southeast Asia.

### 3. China-origin drug assets are now a global pharma pipeline; India's are not

**What China did:** GSK licensed a multiple-myeloma T-cell engager from Chengdu's Chimagen Biosciences for up to US$750m. That is the small end of the trend — 2026's China-to-West licensing includes AstraZeneca/CSPC at up to US$18.5bn and BMS/Hengrui at up to US$15.2bn, with average biopharma deal size up ~76% year on year to around US$1.3bn, largely on China-originated assets.

**Where India stands:** India is the world's pharmacy by volume and a formidable CDMO and generics base, and Indian firms do license *in* and manufacture at scale. What is conspicuously absent from 2026's mega-licensing tables is Indian-*originated* novel biologics being bought by big pharma at nine or ten figures. Biocon, Sun and Zydus have real NCE programmes; none are producing deal flow at Hengrui/CSPC scale.

**Why it matters:** This is a discovery-capital and clinical-data gap, not a talent gap. For a software founder it is not directly addressable — but it does mark where Indian pharma's own spend is heading, and services sold into that shift (trial-data tooling, regulatory-submission automation) have a buyer with money.

### 4. Purpose-built freight infrastructure: the Pinglu Canal

**What China did:** Opened its first new canal since 1949 — 134.2km from Nanning to the Gulf of Tonkin — removing a 560km detour via Guangzhou for inland Guangxi cargo, with new freight routes straight to Vietnam's Can Tho and Hainan's Yangpu.

**Where India stands:** Honest nuance — India is *not* absent on inland water transport. National waterways cargo reached about 198 million tonnes by February 2026 across 32 operational waterways, and the Jal Marg Vikas Project on the Varanasi-Haldia stretch was around 82% complete mid-2026. The difference is in kind: India is dredging and equipping existing rivers; China built a new channel where geography didn't provide one. India's waterway share of freight is still around 2%, targeted at 5% by 2030.

**Why it matters:** Treat this one as a modest gap, not a chasm. The transferable lesson is that China optimised a *route*, not a river — and the commercial opening in India is less in civil works than in the logistics-software layer that makes multi-modal river freight actually schedulable.

## 🤖 AI Monetization Opportunities for India

### 1. An AI token-spend governance layer for Indian IT services and GCCs

**The signal:** Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent and ByteDance have all moved from "use AI constantly" to hard token quotas in a single year — ByteDance now reimburses only about half of external AI tool spend, capped near US$1,000/year for engineers and US$300 for non-technical staff. The enthusiasm phase ended at the finance department.

**The concept:** A FinOps-for-AI control plane aimed at Indian IT services firms and global capability centres: a proxy that sits between employees and model providers, attributes every token to a person, team, client and billing code, enforces per-role budgets, auto-routes cheap requests to smaller models, and produces the client-chargeback report. Indian IT services bills clients by the hour and is now expected to absorb model cost inside fixed-price deals — nobody there currently knows which client's work burned which tokens.

**Indian market & monetization:** B2B SaaS, per-seat or percentage-of-spend. Target segment: mid-tier IT services (Mphasis, Persistent, Coforge tier) and the ~1,700 GCCs in Bengaluru/Hyderabad/Pune, where the buyer is a delivery head with a P&L and a compliance requirement. Land with a single delivery unit, expand to the enterprise. Percentage-of-spend pricing is the better model — it aligns with the customer's actual pain and grows automatically.

**Feasibility:** Strong for a small team. It's a proxy, a metering store and reporting — no model training, no hardware, no regulator. The competitive risk is real though: model providers ship native usage dashboards, and LiteLLM/Helicone occupy the open-source end. The defensible wedge is the *client-chargeback* piece, which is specific to the Indian services billing model and which no global tool builds for.

### 2. An agent-payments mandate layer built on UPI AutoPay

**The signal:** Ant International open-sourced the Agentic Mobile Protocol so AI agents can transact as trusted actors across 1.5 billion wallet accounts. Nothing equivalent is published for UPI.

**The concept:** A developer-facing "agent wallet" SDK: an Indian merchant or agent startup embeds it, and it handles the whole mandate lifecycle on top of UPI AutoPay and e-mandates — scoped spending authority ("this agent may spend ₹5,000/month with these merchant categories"), per-transaction attestation of which agent acted and under whose instruction, an immutable audit trail, and instant user revocation. Sell the hard part — consent, limits, disputes and proof — that every agentic commerce startup otherwise rebuilds badly.

**Indian market & monetization:** Per-transaction basis points or per-API-call pricing, with a flat platform fee for enterprise. Target segment: agentic-commerce and travel/quick-commerce startups first, then ONDC network participants who need a shared answer to "who authorised this purchase".

**Feasibility:** Technically well within a small team's reach, but flag clearly: **this is regulated fintech.** You will need a PA/PSP partner bank, you are exposed to RBI's position on mandates and delegated authentication, and the rules for agent-initiated payments aren't settled. Build it as an SDK on a licensed partner's rails, not as a payments licensee. The regulatory timing risk is the single biggest threat to this idea — and also the moat if you get the compliance architecture right early.

### 3. Vernacular AI distributed through existing apps, not a new app

**The signal:** Morgan Stanley's finding is the whole thesis — 80% weekly consumer AI use in China vs 54% in the US, driven not by better models but by embedding AI inside super apps people already open. China won consumer AI on *distribution*.

**The concept:** India has no super app, but it has high-frequency apps with enormous non-metro reach — PhonePe, Paytm, IRCTC, Jio apps, regional commerce and agri platforms. Build an embeddable vernacular AI assistant SDK: voice-first, Indic-language-native, tuned to one high-value workflow per host app (understanding a bill, filing a claim, checking a scheme's eligibility, comparing a loan), running small models at a price point that survives ₹-per-interaction economics. You are the AI layer these apps can't staff for; they are your distribution.

**Indian market & monetization:** B2B2C — the host app pays per monthly active user or per resolved interaction, because the assistant deflects support cost and lifts conversion. Target segment: tier-2/3-heavy consumer apps and BFSI players whose support costs scale with vernacular users.

**Feasibility:** Achievable for a strong small team, and the cost curve is moving your way. Two honest cautions: the host apps are increasingly building in-house, so you need either a genuine model-quality edge in Indic languages or speed they can't match; and voice in noisy, low-bandwidth, code-mixed conditions is genuinely hard engineering — that difficulty is your moat if you solve it, and your failure mode if you underestimate it.
