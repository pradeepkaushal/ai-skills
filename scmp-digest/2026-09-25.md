## 🇨🇳 China Moves India Hasn't Matched Yet

Today's news is dominated by the summit, so there are only three real capability gaps. The cross-border CBDC, advanced-packaging and battery-diversification gaps from yesterday's digest still apply and aren't repeated here.

**1. Tourist payments that work anywhere, open to everyone**
- **What China did:** Tencent launched TenPayGo. A foreign visitor registers with just an email, links any of 7 card networks or about 60 home-country wallets, and can then pay at the tens of millions of merchants on WeChat Pay's network. Alipay has offered similar access for some time. Paying in China used to be a well-known pain for tourists; the two big platforms have largely fixed it.
- **Where India stands:** NPCI's **UPI One World** prepaid wallet lets foreigners pay at UPI QR codes without an Indian bank account or phone number. But in 2026 it was offered mainly at events such as the AI Impact Summit in February. Loads are capped at ₹25,000 per transaction and ₹50,000 a month, and it runs through a few PPI issuers. NPCI says it wants to make the wallet permanent for inbound tourists before the 2026-27 winter season. The gap is not in the payment network itself, where UPI is arguably ahead. It is in how easy it is for a tourist to sign up and how much they can spend.
- **Why it matters:** India is a cash-light economy, but it is still hard for a visitor to use. Each season this stays unfixed costs tourism spending and weakens India's claim that UPI is a global model. It also points to a product opportunity (below).

**2. A home-grown new drug proven in a large phase 3 trial**
- **What China did:** Chinese researchers ran a phase 3 trial of about 1,000 patients at 32 hospitals and published it in JAMA. Their new drug, loberamisal, protects brain cells after a stroke. It stretches the treatment window from 6 hours to 48 hours, and 70% of treated patients regained full independence, 13 points better than standard care.
- **Where India stands:** India is the world's leading supplier of generic drugs but has produced very few new molecules of its own. Examples include Zydus's saroglitazar and Wockhardt's recently approved antibiotic nafithromycin. Large Indian-led phase 3 trials published in top journals are rare. *(This is from my general knowledge; I didn't check it with a search today.)*
- **Why it matters:** Stroke is a leading cause of death and disability in India, and most patients live far from a hospital that can treat them within 6 hours. A 48-hour drug would suit India's geography better than it suits China's. India will probably end up licensing it rather than having developed it. The policy lesson: China's move from making generics to discovering new drugs came from large, fast multicentre trial networks, which India has not built.

**3. Scale and focus of outbound investment**
- **What China did:** China's outbound direct investment reached a record **US$214 billion** in 2025, making it the world's second-largest source of investment capital. Investment in IT and software abroad rose 88% to US$13.1 billion. As US-bound money fell 71%, the rest was redirected to other markets.
- **Where India stands:** Indian companies' actual direct investment abroad was about **US$33 billion** in FY26, with commitments of about US$48.6 billion (RBI data). That is roughly one-sixth of China's, and Indian flows go largely to holding structures in Singapore, Mauritius and the Netherlands rather than to strategic capacity abroad.
- **Why it matters:** China uses outbound capital to secure market access and supply chains, such as battery plants in Hungary and data centres in Europe. Today's opinion piece on diasporas makes a related point: India lets its overseas capital and talent strengthen other economies. Indian SaaS companies selling abroad will increasingly compete against Chinese firms that bring local capital and local data centres with them.

## 🤖 AI Monetization Opportunities for India

**1. An MDR fee auditor and payment-routing advisor for UPI merchants**
- **The signal:** From 15 October, merchants pay a 0.4% fee (capped at ₹300) on UPI payments above ₹2,000. It is the first time UPI costs merchants anything, and SCMP frames it as a test of whether UPI can pay for itself. Millions of mid-size merchants will now get a new line on their settlement reports that they have never had to check.
- **The concept:** A product that reads a merchant's PSP settlement files, bank statements and POS or ERP exports. An LLM-assisted reconciliation engine then (a) checks that every fee was charged correctly (the threshold, the ₹300 cap, exempt categories), (b) shows how much the new fee costs by store, SKU and ticket size, and (c) recommends the cheapest way to accept each type of payment (UPI, RuPay credit on UPI, cards, or payment links for B2B invoices) and whether the merchant should renegotiate with their PSP. It advises only and never touches money.
- **Indian market & monetization:** B2B SaaS at roughly ₹1,500-5,000 per outlet per month. Target mid-size multi-outlet retailers, pharmacy and electronics chains, D2C brands and B2B distributors, where tickets are usually above ₹2,000. An alternative is to charge 15-20% of the overcharges it recovers. Accounting partners (CA firms, Tally resellers) would be an efficient sales channel.
- **Feasibility:** Good for a small team. It is mainly ingesting files and reconciling them, plus an LLM to read messy settlement formats. It doesn't need a payments licence because it neither holds nor moves money. Risks: the fee rules may change before or after launch (build them as configuration), and PSPs may bundle free analytics. The edge has to be neutrality across PSPs.

**2. An AI concierge for foreign tourists, built on UPI One World**
- **The signal:** TenPayGo shows China making payments easy for tourists before APEC. India's equivalent, UPI One World, works but is mostly offered at events, is capped at ₹50,000 a month, and NPCI says it will become permanent for this winter's tourist season.
- **The concept:** A WhatsApp-first, multilingual AI travel companion for foreign visitors. It explains prices ("is ₹800 fair for this auto ride?"), reads signs and menus, books verified guides, drivers and experiences, and helps the visitor sign up for UPI One World through a partner PPI issuer, so paying happens in the same chat. Later it could tell the visitor when they are near their monthly spending cap and pull receipts together for GST refunds or expense claims.
- **Indian market & monetization:** A 10-15% commission on bookings from guides, drivers and experiences, plus a revenue share from the PPI partner on wallet sign-ups. Hotels could white-label it as a B2B product for their guests. Start with the Golden Triangle, Goa and Kerala, where most foreign visitors go.
- **Feasibility:** Medium. The AI part (translation, price checks, conversation) can be built now. The hard parts are signing up trustworthy local suppliers, and the fact that payments depend on a regulated PPI partner. You can't issue the wallet yourself, so partner with one of NPCI's authorised issuers. Tourism is seasonal, and whether NPCI rolls out One World permanently decides much of the payment side. Validate that before building payments into the product.

*Considered and left out:* AI stroke triage in response to the loberamisal news. Qure.ai and others already sell AI tools that read CT scans for stroke in India, and a longer treatment window lowers, rather than raises, the need for faster triage.
