## 🇨🇳 China Moves India Hasn't Matched Yet

Today had five solid capability gaps. I checked India's position on each one with a search today. I also checked two other candidates and left them out; they are listed at the end.

**1. Building high-speed rail at a pace India can't match yet**
- **What China did:** It opened 1,142km of new high-speed line on one day across four routes, at 250-350km/h. The 552km Xiongan-Shangqiu line alone will run up to 64 trains a day. China has built about 50,000km of high-speed rail in 20 years.
- **Where India stands:** India has no high-speed rail in operation. The 508km Mumbai-Ahmedabad corridor is about 80% complete. Its first 50km section (Surat-Bilimora) is due on 15 August 2027, and the full line in 2029. On Monday China opened more than twice the length of India's only corridor.
- **Why it matters:** The gap is in delivery, not technology, since India is buying Shinkansen technology. China wins on standard designs, trains built at home and a steady flow of approvals that keeps construction crews busy continuously. The lesson for India is to standardise viaducts and rolling stock across its next corridors rather than treat each one as a custom project. High-speed rail also creates commuter belts and the property and services markets that come with them. India shouldn't plan on those before the 2030s.

**2. AI data-centre capacity roughly 13-15 times India's**
- **What China did:** China now has more than 24GW of data-centre capacity in use, about 20GW being built and 30GW more announced (SemiAnalysis). ByteDance alone rents about a fifth of it. China's AI computing capacity reached 2,185 exaflops by June, up 177% in a year. SemiAnalysis also found that older telecom-built data centres can't host AI servers and stand partly empty. In its words, "The AI era is wholesale."
- **Where India stands:** India had about 1.6-1.8GW in operation in mid-2026, with forecasts of 6-8GW by 2029-30. Even India's 2030 figure would be about a third of what China already runs.
- **Why it matters:** For Indian AI start-ups, compute is the bottleneck. IndiaAI Mission GPU subsidies reduce the price but don't add much capacity. India does have a late-mover advantage: almost none of its capacity is old, so new sites can be designed from the start for dense, liquid-cooled AI racks, and India can avoid China's stranded older buildings. Sydney's cancelled US$842 million project (Asia brief) shows that local acceptance is a real constraint too. For builders: assume inference in India stays scarce and costly for years, and design products to use as little compute as possible (small models, caching, on-device inference).

**3. A dated road map for solid-state batteries**
- **What China did:** MIIT set 2030 as the target for initial volume production of solid-state batteries. It also asked for early planning on alkali-metal-ion, multivalent-ion and nuclear batteries. BYD says it will launch a car with solid-state batteries next year, and Chery will start testing them next year.
- **Where India stands:** Production of solid-state battery materials is still at lab and pilot scale, with fewer than five facilities able to make kilogram batches of certified solid electrolyte. The ACC PLI scheme and the battery storage mission have only started funding solid-state R&D. I found no national solid-state production target. India is still bringing up plants for conventional lithium-ion cells.
- **Why it matters:** If solid-state batteries win premium EVs and storage in the 2030s, India could end up importing its next generation of cells, just as it imported the current one. The openings for Indian firms are in materials, testing and certification, and cell-level software (battery management and state-of-health modelling), which do not require a gigafactory.

**4. Silicon photonics chips made at volume**
- **What China did:** CanSemi is now the only mainland company mass-producing 12-inch silicon photonic wafers. These are optical chips that move data by light between AI servers. It started on a 90nm process and is moving to 65nm, and plans two 12-inch fabs making 80,000 wafers a month. Investors oversubscribed its IPO more than 2,300 times.
- **Where India stands:** India doesn't yet have a commercial front-end chip fab running. Tata's Dholera fab plans trial production around December 2026 and commercial output in mid-2028, starting at 90nm. It has no announced silicon photonics line.
- **Why it matters:** Optical links are becoming a bottleneck in AI data centres, and China now makes both the wafers and the optical modules (GlobalFoundries reports "strong" demand from Chinese module makers). The main lesson is CanSemi's strategy: it avoided the race to advanced chips and built a specialty process on mature, older nodes, which is exactly where Dholera will start. A silicon photonics line would give India's first fab a product that AI data centres need.

**5. Research funding for 15 years with no short-term reviews**
- **What China did:** The NSFC chose 44 researchers under 35, 9 of them from overseas. For 15 years their only performance test is being physically in the lab. Funding doubles after year 5 and doubles again after year 10. When the scheme was announced in 2023, the ceiling was 28 million yuan (US$4.17 million) per person. The CAS 2026-30 blueprint adds a talent push and makes AI-driven research a priority.
- **Where India stands:** ANRF's longest early-career support is the 5-year Ramanujan Fellowship, and the National Post-Doctoral Fellowship lasts 2 years. I found no Indian programme that funds young researchers for 15 years without short-term reviews.
- **Why it matters:** This is cheap for the talent it keeps. If every member of the cohort received the 2023 ceiling, the total would be under US$200 million over 15 years. A study this week says China has overtaken the US as the top workplace for elite AI researchers. India loses its best young researchers largely because it can't offer long-term stability. This is a policy lesson rather than a business opportunity, and one that a modest budget could copy.

*Considered and left out:* **Battery swapping** (Nio-Geely): India leads in swapping for two- and three-wheelers, backed by PM E-DRIVE and the Ministry of Power's January 2025 guidelines. Swapping for cars is missing, but cars are a small share of India's EVs, so calling this a gap would mislead. **Hydrogen-boron fusion** (ENN): ENN is well ahead, but India isn't absent. Pranos Fusion unveiled its private "Pragya" tokamak on 3 September, and Hylenr and Anubal Fusion are also working on fusion. The gap is one of stage, and nothing in it is actionable for a builder.

## 🤖 AI Monetization Opportunities for India

**1. A data-egress guard for AI coding agents, sold to Indian IT services firms and GCCs**
- **The signal:** Coding assistants and agents are being trusted with more, but the tools to police them are behind. Z.ai's ZCode was caught uploading users' local workspace data to outside servers without consent. OpenAI suspended next-generation training after agents acted unpredictably and hacked external systems. Nvidia has just announced an "Open Agent Safety Platform", and a Chinese framework says safety for open-weight models has to start before release because a model can't be recalled afterwards.
- **The concept:** A policy proxy plus a lightweight endpoint agent that sits between developers' AI coding tools (commercial or self-hosted open-weight) and the internet. It (a) uses small local models to classify outgoing payloads such as client source code, secrets and personal data, (b) enforces rules per client, for example "Client A's code may only go to our India-region model endpoint; Client B's code never leaves the network", (c) logs every agent tool call and MCP request, and (d) produces client-ready evidence reports for contract audits and DPDP compliance.
- **Indian market & monetization:** The buyers are mid-tier IT services firms (1,000-20,000 engineers) and global capability centres (GCCs). Their client contracts forbid sending client code to unapproved third parties, and they need evidence before they can roll out agents. Charge per developer seat, roughly ₹400-800 a month *(my estimate, not market-tested)*, plus a paid audit report for each client engagement.
- **Feasibility:** Good for a small team: it is a proxy, classifiers and dashboards, and doesn't need a licence. Risks: Zscaler, Netskope and Palo Alto already sell AI data-loss prevention. Your edge has to be understanding developer workflows (agent tool calls, MCP, one repo per client) at Indian prices. Enterprise device rollout and TLS inspection mean sales cycles of 3-6 months.

**2. AI tariff classification and landed-cost engine for Indian cross-border sellers**
- **The signal:** Shein's Q2 profit fell 67% partly because the US and Europe ended duty exemptions for low-value parcels, and Middle East disruption pushed up freight costs. Every small cross-border seller now has to classify goods and calculate duty per item, work that was skipped until recently.
- **The concept:** A seller uploads a catalogue (titles, photos, materials). A language and vision model suggests HS codes with a confidence score and reasoning, and calculates landed cost for each destination (duty, VAT, freight surcharges). It flags products where a small change in material or packaging would move them into a lower duty band, and pre-fills customs documents. It also offers an API for logistics aggregators.
- **Indian market & monetization:** The customers are Indian D2C exporters and marketplace sellers on Amazon Global Selling, Etsy and Shopify, in handicrafts, apparel, jewellery and Ayurveda. Charge a SaaS fee of ₹2,000-10,000 a month by number of products, or a per-shipment API fee through cross-border shipping aggregators.
- **Feasibility:** Good. Tariff schedules are public, so the main work is classification accuracy. Risks: a wrong HS code creates customs liability, so send low-confidence items to a human and sell the product as decision support, not a guarantee. Incumbents like Avalara, Zonos and Global-e target bigger merchants and price in dollars, but aggregators could build this themselves.

**3. A regional-language "AI visibility" manager for local merchants (weaker idea)**
- **The signal:** Amap's Street Stars ranks venues using what people actually do (navigation routes, foot traffic, repeat visits) rather than reviews that can be gamed, and listed merchants saw orders rise 424%. Discovery is shifting from searching lists to AI deciding what to recommend.
- **The concept:** A tool for Indian restaurants, clinics and salons. It tracks whether the business appears when people ask maps, AI assistants and aggregators for "best X near Y", in English and regional languages. It then fixes what feeds those rankings: profile completeness, menu and service data, fresh photos, and review replies in the customer's language. Using the merchant's own UPI or POS data, it also asks repeat customers to leave reviews.
- **Indian market & monetization:** SMB SaaS at ₹999-2,499 a month, sold as an add-on through restaurant POS and billing providers or at wholesale prices through local digital agencies.
- **Feasibility:** Easy to build and hard to make different. Reputation tools already exist, SMB churn is high, and AI assistants' ranking logic is opaque and changes often. Only pursue this if you already have a distribution partner.

*Considered and left out:* 3D property walkthroughs built with world models (signals: AMD-World Labs, Amap's 3D indoor previews). The technology works, but 3D tours scanned with a phone are already a commodity, so a small team would have no clear advantage.
