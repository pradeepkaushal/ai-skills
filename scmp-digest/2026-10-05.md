## 🇨🇳 China Moves India Hasn't Matched Yet

Today's list is short and specific. Ping An's board-approved AI rules looked like a gap but aren't. India's RBI got there first with its FREE-AI report (August 2025), and its June 2026 draft guidance would require every lender to have a board-approved framework for managing the risk of all its models. That story appears under AI idea #1 instead.

### 1. One plant that makes hydrogen and fresh water from seawater
- **What China did:** The Dalian team captures waste heat from electrolysis and uses it to desalinate seawater. Their 250kW plant makes ultra-pure hydrogen and spare fresh water, and the leftover brine can yield uranium and bromine. It uses electricity 14.4% more efficiently than standard alkaline electrolysis and survived 40 days of daily start-stop cycles. Modelled cost is US$2.1/kg with onshore wind power and US$2.9/kg with solar.
- **Where India stands:** India isn't absent, but it is a step behind on the method. Its closest project is IIPE and NTPC's 1-tonne-per-day plant at NTPC Simhadri in Visakhapatnam (about ₹35 crore), which electrolyses water from an existing desalination plant. That is the standard two-step route the Chinese team improved on. India's plant is bigger (the Chinese unit makes roughly 0.1 tonne a day), but it doesn't reuse the waste heat. NTPC also lists direct seawater electrolysis as a research project. ([IIPE–NTPC project](https://www.gleaf.in/news/indias-1st-desalination-based-hydrogen-plant-in-the-making) · [NTPC seawater electrolysis](https://ntpc.co.in/about-us/corporate-functions/et-pr/project-initiative/direct-sea-water-electrolysis) · [Nature Energy paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-026-02130-6))
- **Why it matters:** India's planned green-hydrogen export hubs are at coastal ports where fresh water is scarce. Electricity is the biggest cost in green hydrogen, so a 14% saving that also yields free water moves the cost per kilogram, the number that decides whether Indian exports can compete. The design is published, so India can license or adapt it.

### 2. Highway EV charging built for holiday peaks
- **What China did:** Highway EV charging reached 28 million kWh on 1 October, a holiday record and 60.4% more than the same day last year. Between January and August China added 11,000 high-power highway chargers, as many as in all previous years combined. It has about 49 million EVs, 13.19% of all its vehicles, and plans to double charging points from 20 million to over 40 million by 2030. [link](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3369570/national-day-travel-surge-drives-chinas-highway-ev-charging-volume-60-new-high)
- **Where India stands:** India had about 27,700 public charging stations in March 2026, by one industry count. The PM E-DRIVE scheme funds 72,300 chargers along 50 national-highway corridors, about 22,100 of them fast chargers for cars, and approved 4,874 more in mid-2026. The figures aren't directly comparable: China counts individual charging points, including private ones, while India counts public stations. Even so, China has at least 100 times as many, and most Indian EVs are two- and three-wheelers. ([PM E-DRIVE approvals](https://www.electrive.com/2026/05/13/india-approves-nearly-4900-ev-chargers-under-pm-e-drive-scheme/) · [India charging status](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/03/28/ev-charging-infrastructure/))
- **Why it matters:** China's holiday figures show the problem India will face once electric cars go mainstream: Diwali and long-weekend peaks will overwhelm highway chargers. India is building its highway network now, while there is still time to plan for those peaks. See AI idea #2.

### 3. Lithium: China keeps adding reserves; India produces none
- **What China did:** Registered resources at Sichuan's Jiada lithium mine rose 70.5% to 2.53 million tonnes of lithium carbonate equivalent. Once permits are in place, the first mining area could produce about 50,000 tonnes of lithium carbonate a year. [link](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3369603/chinese-lithium-mine-registers-wider-vein-bolstering-beijings-battery-supply)
- **Where India stands:** India produces no lithium. The government has twice cancelled the auction for the Reasi block in Jammu and Kashmir (5.9 million tonnes inferred) because no qualified bidders came forward. Investors are wary partly because no study yet shows the ore can be processed. The Katghora block in Chhattisgarh, won by Maiki South Mining, is still being drilled to map the deposit. ([Reasi auction](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/2nd-lithium-auction-in-reasi-distt-fails-to-attract-bidders/) · [Katghora status](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/state/countrys-first-lithium-mine-in-korba-to-become-operational-soon-drilling-underway-enn25111601802))
- **Why it matters:** India's battery and EV push depends on imported cells and chemicals, and without its own lithium mining and refining it remains an assembler. This is capital-heavy and offers little for a software team, apart from AI for analysing exploration data.

### 4. Turning R&D into patent royalties
- **What China did:** Huawei's patent-licensing contracts have passed US$6.9 billion since 2011, across more than 260 deals, and Qualcomm is now a licensee. Huawei holds 165,000 active patents, charges 50 US cents per device for Wi-Fi 7, and its licences cover 1.6 billion devices. It spent 192.3 billion yuan (US$28.7 billion) on R&D in 2025, about 22% of revenue.
- **Where India stands:** Indian companies mostly pay licence fees rather than collect them. Jio leads with 37 patent families declared essential to 5G standards; other Indian companies have only a handful. ([India 5G SEP landscape](https://insights.greyb.com/5g-patents-in-india/))
- **Why it matters:** Every phone assembled in India under the production-linked incentive (PLI) scheme pays royalties to foreign patent holders such as Qualcomm, Ericsson and Nokia, and now Huawei. Huawei shows the full cycle: heavy R&D leads to patents in global standards, and their royalties fund more R&D. India's manufacturing push has the factories but not the patents.

## 🤖 AI Monetization Opportunities for India

### 1. Model-risk compliance for mid-size lenders
- **The signal:** Ping An Bank's board approved rules covering data security, algorithm risk, ethics review and tracing responsibility for its 450-plus AI use cases. Analysts expect other banks to follow. India's version is coming: the RBI's draft model-risk guidance (24 June 2026; consultation closed 24 July) would require every bank, NBFC and other regulated lender to have a board-approved framework covering all its models, from rule engines and machine learning to generative AI and vendor models. The draft also requires lenders to validate third-party models themselves, whatever certification the vendor provides. ([RBI draft analysis](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/06/rbi-draft-model-risk-management-guidelines-2026/) · [FREE-AI report](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/PublicationReport/Pdfs/FREEAIR130820250A24FF2D4578453F824C72ED9F5D5851.PDF))
- **The concept:** A model register with built-in validation, sold as one product. It does four things:
  1. It finds every model a lender uses, from code repositories, API-gateway logs that show LLM calls, and vendor contracts.
  2. It assigns each model a risk tier.
  3. It runs standard tests: drift, stability, bias across borrower groups, explanations for credit decisions, and prompt-injection and hallucination checks for GenAI chatbots.
  4. It produces validation reports ready for the board's risk committee, plus emergency switch-off procedures.
- **Indian market & monetization:** Big banks will build this themselves or hire the Big Four. Target mid-size NBFCs, small finance banks, urban co-operative banks and digital lending partners. The rules will cover them, but they have no model-risk team. Charge an annual subscription priced by number of models, plus a fee for each validation of a high-risk model. There is a second market: AI and fintech vendors selling to banks. Every bank customer must validate the vendor's models anyway, so a ready-made validation pack shortens their sales cycle.
- **Feasibility:** A small team can build this as pure software, using open-source validation and LLM-evaluation tools. There are real risks:
  - The guidance is still a draft, and the final rules could change scope or deadlines.
  - You are selling to regulated buyers with slow procurement.
  - Established governance, risk and compliance software vendors will add this as a feature.
  
  Your edge has to be templates built for the RBI's rules and speed to market. Sign one NBFC as a design partner before building out features.

### 2. Demand forecasting and site planning for highway EV charging
- **The signal:** Highway charging in China jumped 60% on the first day of the holiday, and Beijing added as many high-power highway chargers in eight months as in all previous years. India is now rolling out tens of thousands of subsidised chargers along 50 highway corridors, and operators such as Tata Power EZ Charge, Statiq, ChargeZone and Jio-bp are racing for the busiest routes.
- **The concept:** A planning and operations tool for charge-point operators. It combines their charging-session logs with highway traffic counts, maps of fuel stations, dhabas and toll plazas, local grid capacity, and India's calendar of festivals and long weekends. From that it forecasts demand at each site by the hour, recommends where to add chargers and how many, and suggests time-of-day prices. A surge mode would warn before Diwali and long weekends so operators can bring in mobile chargers or open queue booking.
- **Indian market & monetization:** Buyers are charge-point operators, oil marketing companies putting chargers at petrol pumps, and highway restaurant and hotel chains deciding whether to host chargers. Charge a monthly fee per site for the operations tool, and a fixed fee per corridor for site-selection studies, which state EV agencies might also buy.
- **Feasibility:** It is software only, and the forecasting itself is routine. The hard part is data: detailed highway traffic data, such as from FASTag tolling, isn't freely available. The first version has to run on operators' own session logs plus public data. There are only about a dozen serious buyers, so this stays a niche B2B product unless it expands to fleet operators and two-wheeler battery-swap networks. Solid, but not huge.

### 3. (Weaker) A paid apprenticeship for junior engineers who work alongside AI
- **The signal:** Hong Kong's government now says openly that AI has hit entry-level jobs because it does a graduate's first work: basic coding and first-line support. Indian hiring reports this year show entry-level IT openings shrinking as firms move to smaller teams that use AI tools. ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/entry-level-it-jobs-shrink-20-25-as-ai-reshapes-hiring-in-india-ws-l-95225.htm) · [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/india-incs-fresher-hiring-sees-steep-drop-amid-ai-adoption))
- **The concept:** Trainees work through realistic tickets in a sandbox alongside coding agents. They are scored on the skills juniors are still hired for: writing clear task specs, reviewing and testing what the agent produces, catching subtle bugs, and explaining their changes. Employers get a verified skills score instead of relying on a degree.
- **Indian market & monetization:** IT services firms and global capability centres (GCCs) pay a placement fee per hire, or engineering colleges in tier-2 and tier-3 cities pay per student.
- **Feasibility:** The technology is easy: an agent sandbox plus an evaluation harness. Distribution and trust are the hard part, in a crowded edtech market that students already distrust. It works only if a few employers agree to hire on the score, so sign them up before writing any code.
