## 🇨🇳 China Moves India Hasn't Matched Yet

Three items looked like gaps but aren't:
- **Gig-worker rules.** China's draft would be its first national rules for platform workers, but India's Code on Social Security already covers them. It took effect on 21 November 2025, and the central rules came on 8 May 2026. Those rules require platforms to register their workers on a government portal within 45 days. The Code also charges platforms 1-2% of annual turnover, capped at 5% of what they pay workers, though the start of that charge may not yet have been notified. China's draft has things India's Code doesn't spell out, such as a monthly pay cycle, a holiday premium and published hour caps. Each country is ahead on different parts. ([Rules summary](https://www.impriindia.com/?p=75715) · [DLA Piper on eShram onboarding](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/india-mandates-aggregator-onboarding-to-eshram-portal-to-expand-social-security-coverage) · [K&S on the contribution](https://ksandk.com/labour-employment/gig-workers-epf-code-social-security-2026/))
- **Hong Kong's AI city brain.** The housing ministry said in December 2024 that all 100 of India's Smart Cities run an Integrated Command and Control Centre. What Hong Kong adds is AI that predicts emergencies and decides which department handles cross-department complaints. ([MoHUA, Dec 2024](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/91-per-cent-of-smart-city-projects-have-been-completed-across-india-mohua-enn24121806690))
- **AI microdramas.** Kuku TV launched AI-generated microdramas in February 2026 and has filed confidentially for an IPO. The gap here is scale, not capability. That story is AI idea #3 instead. ([THR India](https://www.hollywoodreporterindia.com/features/insight/indias-microdrama-boom-how-data-desire-and-aritifical-intelligence-are-reshaping-entertainment))

### 1. Memory chips: China is designing next-generation DRAM; India assembles memory made elsewhere
- **What China did:** CXMT has shown a 4F² DRAM cell built on its own vertical transistor, the same design Samsung and SK Hynix are chasing, and plans a server memory module using it by the end of 2026. It already runs a fifth-generation DRAM process in volume and holds more than 300 patent families on the structure.
- **Where India stands:** India has no memory fab. Micron's Sanand plant, which opened on 28 February 2026, only assembles and tests memory from wafers made elsewhere. It does real work: its first India-made modules went to Dell for laptops built in India. India's first wafer fab, Tata's Dholera plant, is still under construction, makes logic chips rather than memory, and will reportedly start at 90nm rather than the promised 28nm. ([Micron Sanand opening](https://www.crnasia.com/india/news/2026/micron-opens-india-s-first-semiconductor-assembly-and-test-facility-in-gujarat) · [Sanand scale-up](https://india.entrepreneur.com/?p=99802) · [SCMP op-ed on Dholera](https://www.scmp.com/opinion/asia-opinion/article/3369950/ai-indias-choice-neednt-be-between-us-leash-or-chinese-hook))
- **Why it matters:** The government expects AI data centres to add 26.3 GW to India's electricity demand by 2031-32. Every DRAM chip in those servers will be imported. Today's laptop story shows the risk: when component prices spike, manufacturers move assembly back to China, where the parts are, which undercuts India's electronics-manufacturing push. Memory fabs cost billions, so there's nothing here for a small software team. The policy point is that assembly and test is a start, not the goal.

### 2. AI agents on the factory floor, and small production runs at low cost
- **What China did:** According to Black Lake, Chinese factories are deploying AI agents for specific roles without first installing traditional factory software. About 40% of its agents already make decisions on their own under human supervision. Its Popzao service cut the minimum order for custom merchandise from 20,000 units to 100. Beijing's "AI plus" plan aims to bring AI into 90% of the economy by 2030. Black Lake is already selling in Vietnam, Indonesia, Malaysia and Mexico, the same countries India competes with for factories moving out of China.
- **Where India stands:** Large Indian manufacturers are building their own systems: Ashok Leyland's Hubble.ai runs predictive models across 150 production stages. A vendor survey claims 40% of Indian manufacturers are piloting AI agents, but treat that as marketing. My search found Indian firms in inspection and robotics (Detect Technologies, Robro Systems, Addverb), but no Indian vendor selling role-based agents across procurement, planning and maintenance to small and mid-size factories. That was a single search, so check before assuming the field is empty. ([Industrial Automation India](https://www.industrialautomationindia.in/articles/indian-manufacturing-agentic-ai-robotics-2026) · [Advaiya](https://advaiya.com/?p=96136) · [PwC MSME projection](https://letsdatascience.com/news/ai-boosts-msme-manufacturing-growth-projections-a76ae3e4))
- **Why it matters:** India's pitch to companies moving out of China has been cost and scale. China now competes on agility, meaning profitable small, customised runs, and it is selling that capability to the countries India is competing against. This is the most buildable gap today; see AI idea #1.

### 3. Original drugs that earn billions abroad
- **What China did:** China already has two drugs it discovered that each sell more than US$1 billion a year worldwide: zanubrutinib (US$3.9 billion in 2025) and Carvykti (US$1.9 billion). Beijing's goal is at least five by 2030. Chinese biotechs accounted for more than half of global biopharma deal value in the first half of 2026. China's share of the world's first-in-class drugs (drugs that work in an entirely new way) has risen from about 4% in 2016 to about 22%, according to DBS.
- **Where India stands:** India leads the world in generics but hasn't reached this point with original drugs. A 2026 BCG/HealthKois report counts more than 10 Indian novel drug assets in a decade. My searches found no India-discovered drug with confirmed sales of US$1 billion a year. Zydus's saroglitazar was forecast to reach that but sells mainly in India. Umbralisib, discovered by Rhizen and the first India-discovered new drug approved by the US FDA (in 2021), was withdrawn from the US market in 2022 over safety concerns. ([BCG/HealthKois via Indian Pharma Post](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/news/10-novel-drug-assets-731-million-in-investments-signal-indias-innovation-shift-report-20940) · [Umbralisib FDA approval](https://www.businesstoday.in/industry/pharma/story/first-new-chemical-entity-discovered-by-indian-scientists-gets-us-fda-approval-287104-2021-02-09) · [Saroglitazar launch](https://scrip.pharmaintelligence.informa.com/SC084783/Indias-First-Unique-LabToMarket-Drug-To-Be-Launched-by-Cadila))
- **Why it matters:** The money in pharma comes from owning the drug, not from making the copy. AstraZeneca's US$2 billion investment tied to ivonescimab is the kind of deal Indian labs aren't getting. A software team can't build a drug, but research-stage biotechs need software: AI for picking which molecules to pursue, and tools for building regulatory filings. That's regulated, specialist and slow to sell into, so only worth pursuing with domain co-founders.

### 4. Turning desalination waste into chemical feedstock
- **What China did:** Tangshan Sanyou's plant concentrates waste brine to 120 g/L, recovers more fresh water from it, and pipes the concentrate 50km to a soda-ash plant. The company says it uses 60% less electricity per unit of output value than conventional desalination. It took hundreds of trials to solve scaling (calcium and magnesium deposits clogging the membranes), and the team chose a Chinese membrane brand over foreign ones. At full build-out it should handle about 100 million tonnes of brine a year.
- **Where India stands:** India's big seawater plants, such as Chennai's Nemmeli and the 400 MLD Perur plant, discharge brine into the sea through diffusers. A 2013 fact-finding report recorded villagers' complaints that Nemmeli released brine onto the beach instead of 650m offshore. My searches found no Indian desalination plant feeding its brine to a chemical plant. The nearest examples are zero-liquid-discharge treatment of industrial effluent (Bodal Chemicals in Gujarat, 2018) and research projects at IIT Madras and CSIR-CSMCRI. The search wasn't exhaustive, so treat this as likely but not certain. ([Perur/Nemmeli brine issues](https://icsf.net/?p=20030) · [Bodal ZLD plant](https://www.watertechonline.com/wastewater/article/16203389/worlds-first-brine-treatment-plant-opens-in-india) · [India desal tech overview](https://indianinfrastructure.com/?p=183837))
- **Why it matters:** India is adding desalination capacity in Chennai and Gujarat, and brine disposal is becoming a coastal environmental and legal problem. Gujarat's coast already has soda-ash plants, such as Tata Chemicals at Mithapur. Linking desalination plants to them would turn a disposal cost into a feedstock and improve desalination economics. This is capital-heavy chemical engineering; the only software angle is process control and membrane-fouling prediction.

## 🤖 AI Monetization Opportunities for India

### 1. A WhatsApp-based planning and purchasing agent for small Indian factories
- **The signal:** Black Lake's main point is that factories don't need traditional factory software before using AI. Agents can work from the messy data factories already have, start as advisers, and take on decisions as they learn from staff corrections. Zhou says an agent catches up with a human in one to two months. China's demand comes from an ageing workforce and more small, customised orders. Indian small factories face the same order patterns, and most run on Tally, Excel and WhatsApp rather than any planning software.
- **The concept:** An agent that works inside the tools the factory already uses. Start with one industry: tier-2 and tier-3 auto-component suppliers in the Pune, Chennai or Rajkot clusters, whose OEM delivery schedules give the agent structured input. Each day it would:
  1. Read incoming purchase orders and schedule changes from PDFs, email and WhatsApp.
  2. Check stock and open orders in Tally.
  3. Propose a schedule for each machine.
  4. Draft purchase orders for raw-material shortfalls and send them to the owner on WhatsApp for one-tap approval.
  5. Chase suppliers for delivery dates.
  6. Flag any customer order likely to miss its date, three to five days ahead.
- **Indian market & monetization:** A subscription per plant of about ₹10,000-40,000 a month, depending on order volume, plus a setup fee. The pitch is that one avoided late-delivery penalty or line stoppage pays for a year. Sell through the Tally partners and chartered accountants who already advise these owners, and through cluster industry associations. Once a few hundred factories use it, a second product appears: supply-chain visibility sold to the OEMs and tier-1 suppliers those factories serve.
- **Feasibility:** Software only, and a small team can build it: an LLM for reading documents, a scheduling solver, a Tally integration and the WhatsApp Business API. The risks are real:
  - Small factories are price-sensitive and cancel often.
  - Their data is incomplete.
  - Owners won't let software place orders without approval, so keep a human sign-off on every order for a long time, as Black Lake does with 60% of its agents.
  - ERP vendors will add AI features.
  
  Spend a month on the floor of two or three factories before writing code. The scheduling logic has to match how the plant really runs, not how a textbook says it should.

### 2. Long-document reliability testing for teams using cheap open models
- **The signal:** The DeepSeek paper shows that a cheaper model can be unreliable in a way normal benchmarks miss: accuracy can swing by up to 40 points depending on where a fact sits in a long document. In today's op-ed, Atreja argues that India's sensible AI path is adapting open models, with an independent evaluator using private Indian-language tests, which India doesn't yet have. Open models such as DeepSeek, Qwen and Sarvam's appeal to Indian teams handling sensitive documents because they're cheap and can run in-house, which is exactly where this failure would go unnoticed.
- **The concept:** A test service run on the customer's own long documents: loan files, insurance claims, court records, tender documents, in English and Indian languages. It plants known facts at different depths, adds irrelevant preambles, and mixes scripts. It then reports where each candidate model fails and whether changing how documents are split or retrieved fixes it. The customer gets a model-selection report and a set of regression tests that rerun automatically whenever they change the model or prompt. This could also serve as the testing engine for the model-risk product in the 2026-10-05 digest (AI idea #1).
- **Indian market & monetization:** A one-off evaluation report (a few lakh rupees) followed by a monthly testing subscription. Target legal-tech and insurtech start-ups, lenders' document-AI teams, and global capability centres building document workflows. If the government creates the independent evaluator Atreja proposes, private Indian-language test sets would make you a natural bidder, but don't count on that.
- **Feasibility:** Software only, and buildable with open long-context tests such as needle-in-a-haystack and RULER, plus rented GPUs to run the open models. General evaluation tools are becoming a commodity, and LLM-monitoring vendors bundle them, so the moat is domain document sets and Indian-language test data, which take time to build. Medium strength: a good business if you can get one legal or insurance design partner to share real documents.

### 3. (Weaker) Fast regional-language versions of hit microdramas
- **The signal:** Over 74% of China's new microdramas are now AI-generated, about 1,500 series a day, and the regulator is pushing AI to cut costs. India's market is following: Kuku TV launched AI-made microdramas in February, StoryTV is the second-biggest app, JioHotstar ran free microdramas during the IPL, and industry estimates put growth in Hindi and other Indian-language microdramas at about 60% a year. One unverified forecast says AI-assisted production could reach about 80% of Indian microdramas within 18 months. ([THR India](https://www.hollywoodreporterindia.com/features/insight/indias-microdrama-boom-how-data-desire-and-aritifical-intelligence-are-reshaping-entertainment) · [Vitrina](https://vitrina.ai/blog/top-micro-drama-series-apps-2026/) · [Kuku IPO filing](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/market-news/kuku-technologies-plans-confidential-3500-crore-ipo-filing-1-8-billion-valuation/))
- **The concept:** Don't compete with Kuku on generating shows from scratch; it does that in-house. Instead, take a microdrama already proven in Hindi and produce Tamil, Telugu, Bengali and Marathi versions within 48 hours. That means adapting the script for each region (not just translating it), dubbing with cloned voices and lip-sync, redoing on-screen text, and adding AI-content labels.
- **Indian market & monetization:** Charge per finished minute, or take a revenue share on the regional versions. Sell to independent studios and smaller apps without their own AI teams, such as STAGE, Hoichoi's Sooper service and the producers who supply the big apps.
- **Feasibility:** Software built on commercial dubbing and lip-sync models, so it's quick to start. That's also why it's weak: there's little to defend, the biggest buyers will build it themselves, Chinese tools are ahead, and every job needs clean dubbing rights from the studio. Worth trying only if you already have relationships with producers.
