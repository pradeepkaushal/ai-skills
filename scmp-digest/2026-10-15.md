## 🇨🇳 China Moves India Hasn't Matched Yet

Today is quiet for real gaps. Most of the China news is diplomacy, trade fights and markets. One item clears the bar, and I checked two more but am not claiming them.

### 1. China builds its own airliner and has a home market buying it
- **What China did:** Comac's C919 is a single-aisle airliner in the same class as the Airbus A320 and Boeing 737, and Chinese airlines have ordered more than 1,000. Cirium forecasts Comac will supply 5% of the world's passenger jets by 2045, up from about 1% today. Deliveries are the problem: Comac delivered 15 in 2025 against a target of 75, and three in the first quarter of 2026. The plane still has no European certification, and reported US restrictions on engine and parts exports are slowing production. Yesterday's digest also covered Beijing's draft rules to make exporting aircraft parts easier.
- **Where India stands:** India has no airliner in production.
  - **SJ-100:** The closest effort is a licence contract HAL signed with Russia's United Aircraft Corporation on 28 January 2026 to build the SJ-100, a Russian regional jet of about 100 seats. UAC says the first India-built plane is about three years away (2028-29), and the Indian version will use Russian PD-8 engines. India's aviation regulator, the DGCA, hasn't yet accepted the plane's Russian certification. In the meantime, HAL plans to lease 10 SJ-100s to Indian airlines ([Interfax](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/115953/), [AIN](https://backend.ainonline.com/aviation-news/aerospace/2026-02-04/hal-and-uac-plot-indian-production-russian-sj-100)).
  - **Military aircraft:** India does assemble the Airbus C295 military transport, at a Tata-Airbus line in Vadodara that opened in October 2024. It is not an airliner.
  - **Foreign orders:** Indian airlines are among the world's biggest buyers of Western jets. In 2023 alone, IndiGo ordered 500 Airbus narrowbodies and Air India about 470 jets (250 Airbus, 220 Boeing) ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/business%2Faviation%2Fairbus-books-record-india-orders-confirms-higher-deliveries-1.96831486), [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/indian-airlines-have-more-than-1-100-planes-on-order-news-262579)). These are 2023 figures, so current order books may differ.
- **Why it matters:** Comac shows what a protected home market can and can't deliver. Orders came easily because state-owned airlines placed them. Deliveries didn't, because the engines and many systems are Western and can be held back. India's SJ-100 deal trades one dependency for another: Russian engines and a Russian certification India hasn't accepted. The lesson isn't that India should build its own C919. Engines and certification are the real bottlenecks, and India's order book for more than 1,000 Western jets gives it leverage to win parts manufacturing, maintenance work and certification expertise. For a software engineer, the near-term openings are in maintenance software, parts traceability and the paperwork needed for DGCA certification, not in building aircraft.

**Checked and not claimed:**
- **Faster stock settlement: India is ahead.** Hong Kong's exchange will probably delay next-day settlement (T+1) to 2028. India completed its move to T+1 in January 2023, and since early 2025 has phased in optional same-day (T+0) settlement for its top 500 stocks ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/sebi-introduces-optional-t0-settlement-for-the-top-500-stocks)). Take-up of same-day settlement has been weak ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/t0-settlement-extension-market-participants-say-it-is-only-a-matter-of-time-before-it-catches-on-449381-2024-10-09)).
- **Domestic AI models on phones: India has a smaller version, by a better route.** China's approval puts Alibaba's Qwen inside iPhones sold in China, because foreign AI there must pass security reviews and register its algorithms, which pushes companies into local partnerships. India has no such requirement, and doesn't need one. Indian models are reaching phones through commercial deals instead. HMD's Vibe 2 5G (₹10,999) ships with Sarvam's Indus chatbot, which handles 22 Indian languages, and sold out on Flipkart within an hour ([FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/483701/hmd-vibe-2-5g-sold-out-first-sale.html/amp)). ## 🤖 AI Monetization Opportunities for India

### 1. A "does this business actually exist?" check for cross-border payment companies
- **The signal:** Bank of America's US$1.21 billion penalty spells out the warning signs it missed:
  - transfers described only as "for goods"
  - counterparties with no web presence operating from homes
  - card spending far above customers' stated income
  - customers calling themselves logistics or electronics firms whose payments didn't fit that business

  The bank had built a tool to detect laundering disguised as trade (trade-based money laundering), but switched it on only for cases already under investigation. Analysts expect Western banks to scrutinise China-linked payments more closely, so Indian firms in those payment chains will face the same questions.
- **Why India:** Trade-based money laundering is an active Indian enforcement target:
  - **Rajeshwar Exports (April 2026):** The Enforcement Directorate (ED), India's financial-crimes agency, searched 13 sites. It alleges that more than ₹1,400 crore was sent to Hong Kong, Dubai and Thailand through over-invoiced imports and fake exports ([ED](https://enforcementdirectorate.gov.in/media/press-release-documents/c6720c39-90c5-40f5-8acc-7b51b4bb55fb_Press%20release_Search-REPL-220426.pdf)).
  - **Yecnail Enterprises (November 2025):** ED filed charges against shell import firms that it says paid for imports at inflated prices to move money abroad ([ED](https://enforcementdirectorate.gov.in/sites/default/files/latestnews/Press%20Release%20-PC-%20Yecnail%20Enterprises%20and%20Others-1.12.2025%286%29.pdf)).
  - **Interpol notice (August 2025):** ED issued its first Interpol Purple Notice, which alerts other countries to a criminal method. The method involved under-invoiced imports and goods shipped around through third countries ([Organiser](https://organiser.org/2025/08/30/313304/bharat/ed-issues-first-ever-purple-notice-via-interpol-how-it-targets-trade-based-money-laundering-differs-from-red-corner/amp/)).

  On the regulatory side, the RBI's September 2025 rules for payment aggregators created a licensed category for cross-border payment firms. These firms must register with FIU-IND, India's financial intelligence unit, check buyers on transactions above ₹2.5 lakh, and cap each transaction at ₹25 lakh ([Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Client-Alert-RBI-Introduces-Consolidated-Framework-for-Payment-Aggregators-3.pdf), [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/rbi-issues-new-master-directions-for-payment-aggregators-rules-take-effect-immediately)). FIU-IND has fined payments firms before, for example ₹5.49 crore against Paytm Payments Bank in 2024.
- **The concept:** An API and a review queue that check counterparties each time a payment is made, not just when a customer signs up. For each payer, payee and invoice, it checks:
  - whether the counterparty has a real business footprint: website, domain age, shipping records, marketplace listings
  - whether its address is residential or commercial, using map data
  - for Indian parties, whether the GST number, importer-exporter code and company registration are valid and match the goods declared
  - whether the payment description fits the declared business (for example, "for goods" from a software firm)
  - whether the invoice's unit price is plausible for that customs product code, to catch over- and under-invoicing

  An LLM reads invoices and payment descriptions. Fixed rules and reference data do the scoring, and every alert shows its evidence.
- **Indian market & monetization:** Sell to licensed cross-border payment aggregators and the banks that sponsor them, to the trade-finance desks of banks licensed to handle foreign exchange, and to fintechs that pay exporters and freelancers. Rough pricing: ₹5-25 per counterparty check with a monthly minimum, or ₹2-8 lakh a month for a large bank.
- **Feasibility:** Software only, so a small team can build it. Most of the data is reachable:
  - GST lookups through licensed GST data providers
  - importer-exporter codes from the DGFT, the foreign-trade directorate
  - company records from the Ministry of Corporate Affairs
  - commercial maps APIs

  Reference prices for invoice checks are the weakest input and usually have to be bought. **Competition is real at onboarding:** Perfios (which owns Karza), Signzy and IDfy already verify GST numbers and company records when customers sign up. The gap is checking counterparties at payment time and comparing invoice prices. **Customers are regulated:** expect slow sales, data-localisation rules and security audits, and false alarms are costly. Sell it as decision support for the compliance team, not as a verdict. Document-reading code could be shared with the 13 October idea for checking exporters against sanctions and export-control lists.

### 2. A map of which suppliers hold your data, for when one of them gets hacked
- **The signal:** One ransomware attack on hosting provider IDC Frontier disrupted 495 Japanese companies and local authorities at once, including JR East. Analysts say AI has removed the protection the Japanese language once gave attackers' targets, and that close links between Japanese companies and their suppliers let one breach spread.
- **Why India:** India has already had its own version:
  - **C-Edge attack (July 2024):** Ransomware at C-Edge Technologies, a joint venture of TCS and SBI, took payment services offline at about 300 small cooperative and rural banks. NPCI, which runs UPI, had to cut C-Edge off ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/india/story/300-small-indian-banks-hit-by-ransomware-attack-payment-systems-temporarily-shutdown-report-439640-2024-07-31)).
  - **CERT-In deadline:** India's cyber-security agency already requires incidents to be reported within six hours.
  - **DPDP deadlines:** Under India's data-protection rules, notified in November 2025, the main duties take effect 18 months later, around May 2027. They include notifying affected people and the Data Protection Board quickly, with a detailed report within 72 hours ([Hogan Lovells](https://ca.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-brought-into-force-), [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.in/globalassets/1.-member-firms/india/assets/pdfs/flyers/privacy-compliance-guide-2025-3.pdf)). Check the exact timing in the rules themselves before building on it.

  When a supplier is hacked, the hard part is working out within hours which of your data it held and whose data that was.
- **The concept:** An AI agent that builds and maintains a map of which suppliers hold which data, using records the company already has:
  - the list of suppliers it pays, from accounting
  - the apps employees log into through single sign-on
  - cloud bills
  - contracts and data-processing agreements, from which an LLM extracts what data each supplier handles and where
  - short questionnaires sent to the teams that own each system

  When a supplier is hit (from news, CERT-In alerts or the supplier's own notice), the tool shows the affected systems, the types of personal data involved and roughly how many people are affected. It then drafts the CERT-In and data-protection notices for a person to approve. Between incidents, it flags concentration risk, for example "41% of customer records sit with one hosting provider".
- **Indian market & monetization:** Sell to mid-size Indian companies that hold personal data but lack big compliance budgets: non-bank lenders, hospital chains, diagnostic labs, edtech, direct-to-consumer brands, and the IT providers that serve cooperative banks. Charge ₹3-10 lakh a year, priced by number of suppliers. Sell through CERT-In-approved auditors and data-protection consultancies.
- **Feasibility:** Software only, built on integrations with accounting tools (Tally, Zoho Books, SAP) and login systems (Google Workspace, Microsoft Entra ID, Okta). **The top of the market is crowded:** OneTrust and Securiti cover privacy compliance, SecurityScorecard and BitSight rate supplier security, and Indian DPDP-compliance startups are multiplying. The opening is fast answers during an incident plus India-specific notices, at a mid-market price. **The notices are legal documents:** have lawyers write the templates and never present the output as legal advice.

### 3. (Weaker) Phone-scan fire-safety checks for older buildings
- **The signal:** Hesai's Kosmo turns buildings into editable 3D models with a handheld scanner. In Hong Kong, a cheap smart-alarm option for old buildings has had no installations in five months because owners can't agree on prices. The technology exists, but checking buildings and agreeing on fixes is the bottleneck.
- **Why India:** Deadly fires keep happening in places that were never properly checked:
  - **Rajkot:** the TRP game-zone fire (May 2024, 27 dead) was at a site operating without fire clearance.
  - **Jhansi:** a fire in a hospital's newborn intensive-care unit (November 2024) killed at least 10 babies.

  Fire departments are short-staffed, and many buildings rarely get a real inspection.
- **The concept:** A tool for fire-safety consultants:
  - Walk the building with a phone, using the depth sensor on recent iPhones or ordinary video.
  - The app builds a floor plan and identifies exits, stairs, extinguishers, hydrants and sprinklers.
  - It checks escape distances and the number of exits against the fire rules in the National Building Code.
  - It produces a draft list of problems, each with a fix and a cost estimate.

  Smart-alarm installations could be sold as a follow-on service.
- **Indian market & monetization:** Sell to fire-safety consultants and licensed agencies that prepare fire-clearance applications, and to hospital chains, coaching centres, schools and hotels. Charge ₹5,000-25,000 per building scan, or a monthly subscription per consultant.
- **Feasibility:** This is weaker for three reasons:
  - The hard part is enforcement, not technology.
  - Measurements must be accurate enough to rely on, and the National Building Code is copyrighted by the Bureau of Indian Standards, so turning its rules into code needs a licence.
  - Fire rules vary by state, and the clearance itself is issued by state fire services, so this can only be a tool for a licensed professional.

  **Missing a hazard creates real liability.** Build it only if you can partner with an established fire-safety consultancy.
