## 🇨🇳 China Moves India Hasn't Matched Yet

**1. Rare-earth-catalysed synthetic rubber at industrial scale**
- **What China did:** SCMP reports rare-earth rubber for EV and heavy-truck tyres is nearing a commercial test as output rises and sells at a premium. Other coverage puts a 100,000-tonne-a-year rare-earth polybutadiene plant (Changchun Institute of Applied Chemistry with PetroChina's Dushanzi Petrochemical) in start-up since July, on a fully domestic technology package. The pitch is lower rolling resistance and better wear for the heavier, higher-torque loads EVs put on tyres.
- **Where India stands:** India is a top-five tyre producer (MRF, Apollo, CEAT, JK) but a net importer of polybutadiene rubber. Indian Oil's 60,000-tonne PBR plant at Panipat, using Goodyear process technology, is only now targeting commissioning around December 2026. Reliance makes PBR under the Relflex brand on conventional catalysts. I found no Indian rare-earth catalyst programme for rubber at pilot or plant scale, and IREL's rare-earth output is small and not geared to neodymium catalysts.
- **Why it matters:** This is China turning a raw-material chokehold into a materials-science product, not just a magnet export. Indian tyre makers selling into EV fleets will buy this input from China or fall behind on rolling-resistance specs. The lesson is the pattern: own the upstream element, then move up into specialty materials. India's rare-earth policy is still stuck at the mining-and-magnets stage.

**2. A home-grown open-source AI agent framework at consumer-app scale**
- **What China did:** DeepSeek Harness, released in August under an MIT licence, has crossed 1.5 million daily and about 5.5 million monthly active users, roughly 92 trillion tokens a day, and about 35,000 third-party plugins. Around 60 per cent of users have built or installed a plugin, and DeepSeek is planning an official plugin marketplace and author programme. The numbers are self-reported and count only official-API users.
- **Where India stands:** Sarvam is the closest analogue, with Sarvam Work, the Samvaad voice-agent platform and the Arya orchestration layer, and claims around 500 enterprise customers and about one crore API calls a day. That is a respectable enterprise business, but it is a closed, sales-led product. No Indian lab has an open agent framework with a developer community or plugin ecosystem of this scale.
- **Why it matters:** The agent layer is where model makers are now trying to capture developers and monetise. DeepSeek is building a Linux-style distribution moat on top of a cheap model. Indian developers are already using Harness, Claude Code and Codex; the gap is that none of the ecosystem value accrues to an Indian platform. Opportunity 1 below is the practical response.

**3. Comfortable all-day non-invasive brain-computer interface with usable typing accuracy**
- **What China did:** A Chinese team reports 94 per cent mind-typing accuracy in day-long wear using non-invasive gradient electrodes, aimed first at everyday monitoring such as epilepsy warnings. For comparison, Meta's non-invasive Brain2Qwerty v2 claims about 61 per cent word accuracy.
- **Where India stands:** Nexstem in Bengaluru sells a 16-channel dry-electrode EEG headset and raised US$3.5 million, IISc launched a "moonshot" brain co-processor programme in March 2026, and Marbles Health does EEG-guided neuromodulation. None of these has published typing or long-duration decoding results. India's position is early research and developer hardware, not clinical-grade decoding.
- **Why it matters:** Non-invasive BCI is the version that can ship without neurosurgery, which makes it the version that matters commercially. If Chinese electrode hardware becomes the reference, Indian neurotech will build on imported sensors. The open space for Indian teams is the software layer: decoding models, Indic-language output, and the assistive-tech integrations.

**4. Field robots that harvest, transport and sort a high-value vegetable**
- **What China did:** Intelligent robots that harvest, carry and grade asparagus made their debut in Lianshui county, Jiangsu, last month. It is a single-county deployment, so this is an early milestone, not a national rollout.
- **Where India stands:** Indian agri-robotics is concentrated in weeding and spraying (Niqo Robotics, Harvested Robotics' laser weeder, AgMove rovers) and autonomous tractors (AutoNxt, Mahindra trials). Segritech's pomegranate-harvesting robot is still in development, though its AI grading machines have a real field result in Gujarat. No Indian robotic harvester is in commercial use.
- **Why it matters:** Harvesting is the hardest and most labour-intensive farm task, and China is attacking it crop by crop. India's farm-labour costs are rising fastest in high-value horticulture, which is exactly where this starts. Hardware is capital-heavy, but the perception and grading software is not, and India's fragmented mandi system makes grading-as-a-service a nearer-term wedge.

*Not counted as gaps today:* the statistics bureau's AI modernisation (India's MoSPI has been running its own survey and CPI-base overhaul), robotaxis (covered in the 5 November digest; India's pod-taxi corridor at Mumbai BKC is under construction and robotaxi launch reports remain unverified), and Huawei's domestic desktop (covered on 6 November).

## 🤖 AI Monetization Opportunities for India

**1. India-specific plugin suite and backend for open agent harnesses**
- **The signal:** DeepSeek Harness has about 35,000 third-party plugins, 60 per cent of its 1.5 million daily users have installed or written one, and an official marketplace with an author programme is coming. Claude Code and Codex have parallel plugin and MCP ecosystems. The plugin layer is becoming a distribution channel in its own right.
- **The concept:** A set of production-grade agent tools for Indian business operations, shipped as plugins across the major harnesses with a shared hosted backend: GST return preparation and reconciliation against GSTR-2B, Tally and Zoho Books connectors, UPI and bank-statement reconciliation, e-invoice and e-way-bill generation, MCA and TDS filings. The plugins are thin; the value is a hardened, audited API that handles the Indian compliance edge cases a general model gets wrong.
- **Indian market and monetization:** Chartered-accountant firms and MSME finance teams pay a per-entity monthly subscription (think Rs 500 to 2,000 per client company) for the hosted backend; the plugins themselves are free to drive adoption. Secondary channel: white-label to accounting-software vendors who want agent access without building it.
- **Feasibility:** High for a two- to four-person team. All public APIs, no hardware, and distribution rides on marketplaces other companies are funding. The main risk is platform dependence, which the multi-harness approach mitigates. Compliance correctness is the moat and the liability, so start with reconciliation and reporting before touching filings.

**2. Water-and-power telemetry and disclosure platform for India's data-centre build-out**
- **The signal:** Batam residents are protesting data centres draining taps; Cop31 opened citing surging AI power demand; US data-centre projects face freezes. In India, capacity has grown from 375 MW to about 1,575 MW since 2020, Karnataka's new policy will map water and energy use by site, a national data-centre policy was announced in October, and no state yet requires centralised water disclosure. Rules are arriving before operators have the tooling.
- **The concept:** A SaaS layer that ingests BMS, chiller and utility-meter data to produce per-site water-usage effectiveness, power-usage effectiveness and off-site water footprints, with pre-built disclosure templates for state policies, BIS data-centre standards and BRSR reporting, plus AI-driven anomaly detection and cooling-optimisation recommendations.
- **Indian market and monetization:** Per-MW annual subscription sold to colocation operators (Yotta, Sify, CtrlS, NTT, STT, AdaniConneX), to hyperscaler India landlords, and to state IT departments that need the aggregate view. Consultancies doing ESG assurance are a channel.
- **Feasibility:** Medium. Software only, but it needs on-site integration partners and one anchor customer to get real data. Regulation is a tailwind rather than a blocker here. Not a weekend project, but a well-scoped two-quarter build for a small team with one infra-ops hire.

**3. China-sourcing price intelligence for Indian MSME importers**
- **The signal:** China's producer prices just posted the biggest rise since 2022 while domestic demand stays weak, and SCMP reports manufacturers are rushing into exports to protect margins. Indian MSMEs importing intermediates (steel products, chemicals, electronic components, machinery parts) will see volatile quotes and aggressive discounting at the same time.
- **The concept:** An AI service that tracks Chinese factory-gate price indices, commodity inputs, freight and the rupee, matches them to a buyer's SKU list, and flags when a supplier quote is out of line with the cost curve or when a category is likely to see export dumping and anti-dumping duty risk in India. Quote-evaluation and negotiation briefs generated per RFQ.
- **Indian market and monetization:** Monthly subscription for import-heavy MSMEs and mid-size manufacturers, with a per-RFQ tier; sourcing agents and customs-house brokers as resellers.
- **Feasibility:** Medium-high. Data is mostly public (NBS, customs, exchange rates) plus the buyer's own quotes; the hard part is SKU-to-index mapping, which is an LLM-friendly problem. No regulatory exposure. The honest caveat is that willingness to pay among small importers is unproven, so lead with mid-size manufacturers who already employ a sourcing team.
