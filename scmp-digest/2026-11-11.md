## 🇨🇳 China Moves India Hasn't Matched Yet

**1. Hollow-core fibre on a 1,000 km real-time long-haul link**
- **What China did:** China Telecom, YOFC and ZTE ran a bidirectional real-time test over 1,007 km of hollow-core fibre, carrying 64 Tbps each way with one-way transit of about 3.43 ms, roughly 30 per cent quicker than solid glass. The stated purpose is tying far-apart data centres together for AI training and inference. The numbers are company-reported via state media, not independently reviewed.
- **Where India stands:** STL announced India's first hollow-core fibre cable in March 2026, a hybrid product aimed at AI data centres. That is a product launch. I found no Indian operator (Jio, Airtel, BSNL) reporting a long-haul hollow-core field trial.
- **Why it matters:** China's "compute in the west, data in the east" model depends on making distant data centres behave like nearby ones. India is heading for the same geography problem as capacity concentrates in Mumbai, Chennai and Hyderabad while land and power are cheaper elsewhere. STL having the cable is a real asset; the missing piece is an operator willing to light a route. Hardware-heavy, so this is a watch item for a software founder, but it shapes where Indian AI compute can sit.

**2. An in-orbit AI computing constellation reaching public markets**
- **What China did:** Adaspace is due to list in Hong Kong on 13 November as the city's first AI-satellite IPO. Its listing profile reports 26 AI computing satellites and 15 completed missions as of 20 October 2026, a first 12-satellite computing constellation launched in May 2025, and a plan for 2,800 computing satellites. The company is loss-making and SCMP says investors are wary.
- **Where India stands:** India is at the pathfinder stage. Pixxel and Sarvam plan a 200 kg Pathfinder orbital data-centre satellite for Q4 2026, TakeMe2Space scheduled its MOI-1A computing satellite on a Falcon 9 for 1 October 2026 (I could not find post-launch confirmation), and Agnikul with NeevCloud is targeting an orbital module by end-2026.
- **Why it matters:** The gap is about two years and one order of magnitude in satellites, which is narrower than most China-India hardware gaps. The lukewarm IPO reception is the more useful signal: orbital compute economics are unproven even for the leader. For Indian teams the software layer (on-orbit inference for earth-observation data, task scheduling) is open and far less capital-heavy than the buses.

**3. A cloud company's in-house AI chip unit going public**
- **What China did:** Alibaba filed to spin off and list T-Head in Hong Kong, following Baidu's Kunlunxin filing in January. Chinese cloud platforms now design their own AI accelerators and are financing them through public markets. Offer size and valuation are not finalised.
- **Where India stands:** Indian AI silicon is at seed stage: Agrani Labs raised US$8 million for an AI GPU design, Calligo is raising up to US$15 million, and an edge-AI SoC from the government-backed design cohort is fabricated on 12 nm at TSMC with volumes targeted for 2027. No Indian cloud or telecom group has a captive AI accelerator in production, and no Indian AI chip designer is near a listing.
- **Why it matters:** T-Head has a guaranteed first customer in Alibaba Cloud, which is what makes the economics work. India's design-linked incentive funds start-ups but there is no domestic anchor buyer committing to deploy Indian accelerators at scale. The policy lesson is demand-side: a captive buyer matters more than another design grant.

**4. EV exports at industrial scale**
- **What China did:** China exported about 582,000 new-energy passenger vehicles in October alone, up 144.7 per cent, and 4.47 million in the first ten months, as petrol prices rose abroad. BYD shipped roughly 165,000 in the month.
- **Where India stands:** India's EV exports are growing fast from a small base: 15,641 units in the April-June 2026 quarter (14 times the year before), with Maruti Suzuki at about 93 per cent of the total and 50,000 cumulative e Vitara exports since August 2025.
- **Why it matters:** This is a nuanced gap, not an absence. India has proved it can export EVs, but one model from one maker carries almost all of it, and China ships in a month about ten times what India has shipped in fifteen. The same fuel-price shock lifting Chinese exports is an opening for Indian makers in the UK, South Africa and right-hand-drive markets, if a second and third exporter show up.

*Not counted as gaps today:* yuan-priced maritime insurance (India's Bharat Maritime Insurance Pool is already writing war-risk cover and a domestic P&I roadmap exists, so both countries are early, though China is further along on currency), the C919 (deliveries are lagging and it still depends on GE engines), and industrial "AI plus" policy (covered under opportunities below).

## 🤖 AI Monetization Opportunities for India

**1. Tabular-AI forecasting layer for mid-size Indian manufacturers and distributors**
- **The signal:** SCMP's "Beyond LLMs" piece quotes Tsinghua's Cui Peng arguing that China's next AI phase is specialist models for structured industrial data, because LLMs are weak on numerical tables. China's "AI plus manufacturing" plan targets 100 industrial datasets and 500 application scenarios by 2027.
- **The concept:** A hosted service that connects to Tally, Zoho, SAP Business One or plain ERP exports and uses tabular foundation models (which need no per-customer training) to produce three outputs a plant owner already wants: SKU-level demand forecasts, reorder and stock-out alerts, and a receivables-delay risk score per customer. An LLM sits on top only to explain results in plain Hindi or English, not to do the maths.
- **Indian market and monetization:** B2B SaaS at roughly Rs 10,000 to 40,000 a month per entity, aimed at auto-component makers, FMCG distributors and pharma stockists with Rs 50 to 500 crore turnover. ERP implementation partners and CA firms are the resale channel.
- **Feasibility:** High for a small team. Software only, no regulated activity, and pretrained tabular models remove most of the data-science cost. The hard parts are messy ERP data and proving accuracy on a pilot customer's own history before they pay. Start with one vertical.

**2. True-price shopping agent for Indian festive sales**
- **The signal:** Singles' Day at 18 is dropping "coupon math" for plain prices plus AI, because shoppers are tired of stacked discounts. A Chinese app where strangers vote on your purchase to curb impulse buying has gone viral. Indian sale events still run on exactly the complexity China is abandoning: bank-card offers, cashback, exchange bonuses, no-cost EMI and coins.
- **The concept:** A browser extension and WhatsApp bot that computes the real payable price of a product across Flipkart, Amazon, Croma and brand sites for the cards and wallets the user actually holds, shows 90-day price history, and says "buy now", "wait" or "cheaper elsewhere".
- **Indian market and monetization:** Consumer freemium with affiliate commission on redirected purchases; a paid tier for price-drop alerts on high-ticket items; later, card-offer analytics sold to banks.
- **Feasibility:** Medium. Buildable by two engineers, but it depends on scraping and affiliate programmes that the marketplaces control and can cut off, and affiliate margins are thin. Existing price trackers cover history but not card-level effective price, which is the gap. Treat it as a distribution play, not a defensible technology.

**3. Cold-chain controller monitoring for pharma and quick commerce (thinner idea)**
- **The signal:** Cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration in 12 EU countries spoiled stock, and the EU cyber chief warns home appliances are next.
- **The concept:** A passive monitor that cross-checks refrigeration controller commands against independent temperature sensors and flags tampering or drift, with AI anomaly detection and audit-ready logs.
- **Indian market and monetization:** Per-site subscription for pharma distributors, vaccine stores and dark-store operators, where a spoiled batch is both a loss and a compliance problem.
- **Feasibility:** Lower. It needs an on-site gateway device and integration work per controller brand, and Indian buyers have not yet had the incident that creates budget. Worth watching; not the one to start with.
